# The brief, the review, and a worked example

## The brief

What you return for one piece, before anyone scripts it. Keep it to a page; it is the team's record of why this video, told this way.

```markdown
# Brief: <working title>

- **Slot:** <long or Short, publishing date>
- **Verdict:** proven | open | reframe | drop. <one sentence why>
- **Measured:** <date, and how: yt-dlp, the Research view, or web search only>

## The evidence
| Views | x | Age | Channel | Title |
|---|---|---|---|---|
<two to five reference videos on this angle, linked>

What they share: <the title shape, the promise in the first ten seconds, the length>.
What none of them does that we can: <the team's honest difference>.

## The angle
<one paragraph: whose question, in their words; what they leave with; where the product comes in, if at all (once, late)>

## Package
- Titles: 1. <...> 2. <...> 3. <...>
- Thumbnail words: <a few>; object: <...>
- Frame 0: <what is on screen>

## The first seconds
<the first two or three lines, and what is on screen under each>

## Structure
| Time | Beat | Re-hook? |
|---|---|---|

## Look
<the channel's look, or the one being tested, and why>

## Facts to check
<each claim about another product, person or number, with the page it must come from>

## Risks
<what could make it flop or mislead, and the guard>
```

## The review

When asked to review a script, a sheet of stills or a cut, return a list, most costly first:

```markdown
1. <the problem, at its time code or line> | breaks: <playbook entry or team rule> | fix: <the change>
```

Then the checklist below, each line passed or failed.

## Before publishing

1. The topic's verdict and reference videos are in the brief.
2. The title is the viewer's question in their words; the promise is kept within 30 seconds (three on a Short).
3. Frame 0 works as the cover; no logo, product screen, tagline or call to action in the first three seconds.
4. The picture changes every one to two seconds in the opening; nothing held still.
5. The product, if shown, appears once, late, named plainly.
6. Every word (title, description, on screen, pinned comment) passed the team's copy rules.
7. Every fact about another product or person carries its source and date.
8. Shorts: related video set, captions on. Long videos: chapters, captions, a title test set up.
9. "Notify subscribers" left ticked.

## A worked example (invented)

Crumb makes an app that plans a small bakery's week. Maya runs its YouTube channel and wants a video called "How Crumb plans a bakery's week". All names and numbers below are invented.

**The check.** The phrasings people would type: "bakery production schedule", "how to plan bakery production", "sourdough schedule full time job". Autocomplete completes the third to "sourdough schedule for working people" and "sourdough schedule 9 to 5". The measurements, on 22 October:

- "bakery production schedule": the top results are two software companies' own videos, at 0.4x and 0.6x their channels' usual. **Weak.**
- "sourdough schedule full time job": three channels at 2x or more with over 20,000 views, two of them under a year old. **Proven.**

**The verdict:** reframe. The company's planning story checked weak; the same facts, told from one baker's week, checked proven.

```markdown
# Brief: A sourdough schedule that fits a 9 to 5

- **Slot:** long, 8 minutes, Monday 2 November
- **Verdict:** reframe. "How Crumb plans a bakery's week" checked weak (vendor videos at 0.4x to 0.6x); "sourdough schedule full time job" checked proven on three channels.
- **Measured:** 22 October, yt-dlp, three phrasings.

## The evidence
| Views | x | Age | Channel | Title |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 310K | 4.1 | 8 months | The Weekday Loaf | My sourdough schedule around a full-time job |
| 95K | 2.6 | 5 months | Rye at Home | Overnight sourdough for 9 to 5 workers |
| 48K | 2.2 | 2 years | Field Flour | The lazy sourdough timetable |

What they share: "my schedule" in the first person, the timetable on screen within ten seconds, 7 to 11 minutes.
What none of them does that we can: plan the week backwards from when the bread is needed, with the timings written down to copy.

## The angle
One home baker with an office job and a Saturday market stall. She leaves with a printable timetable. Crumb appears once, at about six minutes, named plainly, as the way Maya's bakery customers keep the same plan.

## Package
- Titles: 1. A sourdough schedule that fits a 9 to 5  2. My sourdough timetable around a full-time job  3. Sourdough on workdays: the whole week, planned
- Thumbnail words: "Mon to Sat"; object: a loaf on a desk calendar
- Frame 0: "Sourdough with a full-time job?" in big words over the calendar

## The first seconds
"You work nine to five. Sourdough takes two days." (the calendar, Monday circled) "Here is the week that makes it fit." (the timetable slides in)

## Risks
The two-year-old reference may describe an older habit; the brief leans on the two recent ones. The product beat must stay under a minute, or it reads as an ad.
```

**What changed because of the check:** the title names the viewer's question instead of the company's process, the opening shows her week instead of the app, and the product moved from the first frame to one beat at six minutes.
