# The playbook: what gets views, with the evidence

Dated 2026-10-22. A living file: the weekly scout adds, corrects and re-dates entries, and an entry not confirmed for 90 days is marked "stale" until someone checks it again. Copy it into the project (for example `video/playbook.md`) and keep it there; the team's own results go in the last section.

Each entry: what to do, the evidence, how strong it is, when it was last checked. **Strong:** YouTube's own statement, or a pattern measured across many channels. **Medium:** one measured case, or a consistent account from working creators. **Weak:** one anecdote or a vendor's claim. **Method:** how this playbook works; it needs no outside evidence. "[checked 2026-10-22]" means re-read at source that day; "[read <date>]" means not re-checked since. Numbers in brackets point to the Sources.

## How YouTube finds viewers

- **A new channel is matched by topic.** With no viewer history, YouTube looks at "what we understand about what this video is about" and the viewer's topical interests; if it finds no interested viewers, "maybe that video just won't get more impressions" [9]. Make it plain, in the title, description, spoken words and chapters, which question the video answers. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Search ranks on relevance, engagement and quality:** how well "the title, tags, description, and video content" match the query, the watch time for that query, and signs of expertise. Payment buys no placement [3]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Nothing is settled in the first hour.** YouTube is "not going to decide just based on the first hour or first 1,000 people"; evergreen videos can collect most of their views a month or more later [9], in "multiple waves" [10]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Each surface judges a video by how it does there.** Home ranking looks at "how well videos perform in that home page context", so viewers sent from a newsletter or a post do not count against it [9]. Send first viewers who already have the question. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Publish steadily.** "Uploading less frequently than usual" is one of YouTube's listed causes of falling Home and Suggested traffic; publish time "is not known to impact a video's long-term performance"; one weak video does not hurt the channel [4]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Leave "notify subscribers" ticked.** Unticking it stops the system connecting "with a subset of viewers who are going to be interested" [9]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Views count from the first frame since 24 August 2026,** autoplay included; an engaged view is a click to watch or watching past the first seconds. Click-through and retention stay based on engaged views [7]. Never compare public views across that date. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]

## Topic

- **Pick from evidence.** A topic is made when the topic check calls it proven or open ([topic-check.md](topic-check.md)). Method.
- **Early in a topic there is "high demand and low supply. So a lot of views per video"; later, supply catches up** [10]. A release the audience will search for is worth a video within days; creators who cover news aim for 48 hours. Before an explainer, check whether a definitive one exists; bring what it lacks or pick another question. Strong (the dynamic); medium (the speed). [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Shapes to look for in your niche's breakouts:** the short course on one thing ("X clearly explained"); the honest test in the first person; levels, rankings and mistakes; a verdict on news within days; "my real setup"; something the viewer can take away (a file, a template). Which of these wins varies by niche; the scout finds out. Method.
- **Original Shorts are favoured.** On 2026-10-16 YouTube said it is changing recommendations to favour original Shorts and cut the reach of channels that mainly re-upload others' clips without significant changes [13]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]

## Title

- **The viewer's question, in their words, one question per video.** Follows from how search matches [3]. YouTube's advice for its conversational search (Ask YouTube) is "relevant titles, detailed descriptions, and clear timestamp chapters" [13]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **The promise is kept in the first 30 seconds.** YouTube reads a high 30-second share as the opening matching what the title and thumbnail led viewers to expect [1]. Ads clear about what they showed were liked at a glance and on longer viewing; ads that first suggested something else were disliked once understood [17]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Plan the title and thumbnail before the video.** The MrBeast production memo: "you must know the title and thumbnails of the videos you are making" [22]; Paddy Galloway, who consults for large channels, says the same and keeps most titles at 50 characters or under [23]. Medium. [checked 2026-10-22 for [22]; [23] read 2026-10-14]
- **Copy a proven title's shape, never its words.** Method.
- **Test titles and thumbnails.** Test & compare runs up to three titles or thumbnails, picks the winner by watch time share, and "should be completed within two weeks"; it needs advanced features, runs from Studio on a computer, and is not available for Shorts [2]. Whole-video A/B tests and per-audience thumbnails were announced on 2026-09-25; check whether your channel has them [14]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Change an old title or thumbnail only if it underperforms:** "don't change what's working" [4]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]

## Thumbnail

- **A glance test:** one object, a few words (Galloway: five or six at most), readable at phone size; title and thumbnail read as one, each adding to the other instead of repeating it [23]. Medium. [read 2026-10-14]
- **The first frame is a second cover.** A Short plays at once in the feed, and Home autoplays openings before many people see the thumbnail, so the first seconds should look like what it promised (MrBeast, with Colin and Samir) [23]. Medium. [read 2026-10-14]

## The opening: never like an ad

- **Long videos are scored at 30 seconds** (the "Intro" figure in Studio) [1]. Confirm the click at once, add something the thumbnail did not promise by about 30 seconds, and stop selling and start showing by about a minute ("Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them" [22]). Strong (the measure); medium (the cadence). [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Feeds judge the first three seconds.** Instagram's skip rate counts who left within three seconds [24]; TikTok tells advertisers to state the proposition in the first three [25]; LinkedIn says intro boards "cut into your six-second window" [19]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22; [24] and [25] read earlier]
- **Open on the viewer's subject, with no logo, product screen, tagline or call to action in the first three seconds.** In zapping and eye-tracking data on TV commercials, a brand in the centre of the screen made people skip more, and the same brand time in brief pulses made them skip less [16]. TikTok's "Don't make ads, make TikToks" says polished brand content has lost its appeal there [18]. LinkedIn's study of 13,000 B2B video ads advises opening on an insight, a statistic or a question, without branding [20]. Medium to strong: independent sources, all from advertising, point the same way. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **The counter-evidence, for paid ads:** a study of 109 B2B ads found branding "early and often" helped people name the brand afterwards [21]. That measures recall in an ad nobody chose. In organic video the viewer decides whether to stay: open on their subject, and show the product late, once, or as a small corner mark. Medium. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **What the openings of channels that hold viewers share:** big words on screen in the first second, near the middle; the picture changes every one to two seconds; one thing on screen at a time; the channel's own name a small mark or nowhere. Medium (seen in the best-performing openings of 24 faceless explainer channels, such as Kurzgesagt and RealLifeLore, October 2026). [read 2026-10-21]

## Holding viewers

| When (long video) | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 s | Confirm the click: the title's promise, on screen, in the viewer's situation. |
| by 30 s | The point, and something the thumbnail did not promise. |
| by 1 min | Stop describing what is coming; show it. |
| every 2 to 3 min | A re-hook: a turn, a surprise, a new question. |
| before halfway | The payoff starts. |
| last 10% | Land it and stop. |

A Short: the subject in big words from frame 0, the promise by three seconds, one re-hook at about 20 to 25 seconds, the payoff before the end.

- **A causal chain.** Between beats there should be "therefore" or "but", never "and then" (Trey Parker and Matt Stone) [26]. Medium. [read 2026-10-14]
- **The payoff in the first half.** The memo: viewers who watch the first half have "a very high chance" of watching to the end [22]; Wistia puts most drop-off around the middle of 1 to 5 minute videos [27]. Medium (a leak and a vendor). [checked 2026-10-22 for [22]]
- **Re-hooks:** a "3 minute re-engagement" [22]; Kallaway's first re-hook at about 20 to 25 seconds in a short, two to three minutes in a long video [28]. Medium (creators). [read 2026-10-14]
- **Short endings.** "The video endings must always be abrupt to protect retention" [22]. Medium. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Read the retention graph** as YouTube describes it: flat means watched through, a gradual decline is normal, a dip is a part people skipped or left, a spike is a part watched again or shared; when the best moments come late, move them earlier [1]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]

## Shorts and long videos

- **Shorts can run up to three minutes** (square or taller) since 15 October 2024 [8]. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Make each Short to stand on its own.** YouTube's Shorts product lead: chopped long-form "tends to perform not as good as when you're purposeful about making content that stands on its own" [11]. Set the Short's related video to the long video on the same question. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Read "stayed to watch"** (formerly "viewed vs swiped away"): "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short" [5]. YouTube publishes no target. Galloway's 2023 study of 5,400 Shorts found those under about 60% viewed rarely did well [29]. Strong (the measure); weak (the threshold, seen only in secondary coverage). [checked 2026-10-22]

## Description, chapters, reach

- **Description:** detailed, naming the question and what the video covers [13]. **Chapters** named by what is inside; they need a first timestamp of 00:00, at least three, each at least ten seconds [6]. Captions from the script. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **Collaborations:** in YouTube's study of February 2026 uploads, creators about 30 times larger than their collaborator averaged a 30% uplift in first-week views on collab videos, similar-sized channels 25% [12]. The smaller channel's gain is not published. Strong. [checked 2026-10-22]
- **YouTube's own research tools:** Studio's new Research view "surfaces outliers" among videos your audience watches [14], in test with a small number of channels in August 2026 [15]. Where you have it, it does part of the topic check for you. [checked 2026-10-22]
