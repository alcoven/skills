# Checking copy and pitch against the positioning

Messaging, the homepage, the pitch, the deck and the ads are written from the positioning. A copy check reads them against the agreed canvas and shows, line by line, where they fit, where they drift and what is missing. The person decides every change.

## What to collect

- The agreed canvas, with its date. If there is none, say so: you can still check the copy for the patterns below, but "off-position" has nothing to measure against. Offer the positioning exercise first.
- The copy as it stands, with where it lives: homepage (first screen and each section), first-call pitch or sales deck, investor deck, ads, store page, sales emails, the one-line description used in profiles and directories.
- What it is for: a first visit, a first call, an investor meeting, a test.

## The first screen

A visitor from the best-fit group should answer five questions from the first screen of the homepage, or the first two minutes of a pitch, without scrolling or asking:

| Question | Canvas section it comes from |
|---|---|
| What is it? | 6, the market category, in words buyers already use |
| Who is it for? | 5, who cares a lot, specific enough that the wrong visitor knows to leave |
| Why is it better than what I do now? | 4, the lead value, against 2, the main alternative |
| Can I believe it? | 4, proof, from customers like the visitor |
| What do I do next? | The ask: one clear next step |

## Trace every claim

Go through the copy line by line and give each a verdict.

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fits | Traces to a canvas line and its proof |
| Unsupported | A claim with no canvas line or no proof behind it |
| Off-position | Says what the canvas chose against: a dropped category's words, an audience left out, a dropped theme |
| Missing | A lead theme, the category or the proof the copy never shows |
| Unfair | Describes an alternative in a way you can't show, or claims a competitor lacks something unchecked |
| Planned | Presents something still in development as available |

Fix order: Planned and Unfair first (they put trust at risk), then Missing on the first screen, then Off-position, then Unsupported.

## Patterns that point to a positioning problem

- A feature list with no "so what" after any item.
- "For everyone", "for teams of all sizes", "for any business".
- Placeholder value: "save time", "all in one", "powerful", "easy to use", with no amount, person or comparison.
- Category words that invite the wrong comparison (the old frame lingering in a headline or a page title).
- "AI-powered" as the headline difference.
- A coined category name with no familiar anchor next to it.
- Proof from customers unlike the best-fit ones (big logos when the target is small firms).
- The company's history or founding story as the opening.
- In a sales pitch or deck: a vast future market, or promises about next year, where the buyer needs value today.
- Criticism of an alternative you can't show, or no mention of the alternative the buyer actually uses.

## The sales pitch storyline

April Dunford's *Sales Pitch* (2023) turns positioning into a first-call story in two halves: a setup that agrees on the buyer's world before the product appears, and a follow-through that shows why you are the best answer in that world. Check a pitch or sales deck part by part.

| Part | What it does | From the canvas | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Insight | A change in the market or the buyer's situation that makes your value matter now | 7, point of view | A view on the market, with evidence; company history doesn't count |
| 2. Alternatives | The approaches buyers use, with what each does well and its limits | 2 | The buyer's real alternatives, doing nothing included, described fairly |
| 3. The perfect world | What an ideal answer would do, drawn from those limits | 2 and 4 | Sets buying criteria your value meets, without naming your product yet |
| 4. Introduction | The product and its category, briefly | 6 | One or two sentences in buyers' words |
| 5. Differentiated value | Each value theme, shown with the capabilities behind it | 3 and 4 | Themes first, then a demo of what proves them, then the themes again |
| 6. Proof | Evidence for each theme | 4, proof | From customers like this buyer; numbers carry their source |
| 7. Objections | The predictable worries, answered before they are raised | 8 and call notes | The objections that recur on calls (switching, price, risk) |
| 8. The ask | A clear next step | (none) | One step, the one the buyer's process needs next |

Watch for the investor story leaking in: Dunford points out that investors reward a vast future and a big change to the market, while customers buy today's value and fear having to replace what they just bought. Keep the vision for the investor deck, and in a sales pitch connect any future view to the step the buyer can take now.

## Ads

- One value theme per ad, aimed at the signs in canvas section 5.
- Ads can test candidate themes or frames before the canvas settles. Clicks and sign-ups show interest; buying and staying are stronger evidence. Record a test's result as evidence in section 8 with its grade.
- If the team tests a planned capability in an ad, note it in the review as a test, and keep it out of the canvas's proof and out of the pitch.

## Test with people

- Show the first screen, once, to five people from the best-fit group (colleagues and friends don't count). Ask: what is it, who is it for, what would you use instead, what is different about it? Write their answers verbatim and compare them with the canvas. Where their words are better than yours, consider using them.
- On sales calls, note whether prospects repeat the lead value back, which objections recur, and any alternative new to the canvas. These go to the next version as evidence.
- Dunford's 2026 advice: test positioning in sales conversations before polishing the website.

```markdown
|---|---|---|

| Line (quoted) | Canvas line | Verdict | Suggested line |
|---|---|---|---|

## Changes to consider

Suggestions only; nothing changes without your yes.

1. Consider [change], because [the line above].
2. [Two or three more at most.]

## Gaps in the canvas this review found

- [A claim the team wants to make that the canvas can't support yet, and how to get the evidence]
```

Crumb's homepage, checked against the canvas in canvas.md (version 2, 14 Oct 2026).

The copy as it stands:

> Take orders, get paid and grow your bakery, all in one place.
> Beautiful shop page · Card payments · Standing orders · Bake sheet · AI-powered
> Trusted by [three shop-only bakeries' logos]
> Start free
