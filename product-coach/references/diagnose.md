# Turning a vague goal into a diagnosis

"How can we make more money?", "we need more customers", "sales are bad", "where do we start?". These are honest questions from people who know their business and may not know the words product people use. The worst answer is a list of tactics. The best is a short conversation that finds where the money or the customers are lost, then one step to take this week. This reference is the method in full.

## The one round of questions

Read the record first. Then ask only what it doesn't answer, at most three questions, each with options the person can pick from. "I don't know" is a fine answer: it tells you what to measure first.

1. **What does it mean for you?** Pick the option list that matches how they asked.
   - For money: a) more money coming in each month, b) more left over after costs, c) cash runs out by a date, d) a target you set and missed, e) it just feels slow.
   - For customers or sales: a) few new customers sign up, b) customers sign up but stop paying after a few months, c) customers stay but use it little, d) a target you set and missed, e) not sure.
   - When one side pays and another uses (a salon and the clients who book), make one option about the users: "the salons stay, but few of their clients book through the app".
2. **Who pays, and who uses it?** Ask only when they may differ: a shop and its shoppers, a school and its students, a company and its staff, a bakery and the people ordering from it.
3. **The rough numbers, if you have them:** how many customers pay today, how many paid three months ago, how many stopped since, and how the last five found you.

Why these three: the first tells you which part of the money map matters, the second tells you whose behaviour to look at, the third usually shows the leak by itself.

## Two cases that change the order

- **Cash runs out by a date.** Count the months of cash left before anything else. Then the fastest money: invoices not yet paid, a yearly plan offered to the customers who use it most, a higher price for new customers only, costs that bring nothing in. Then the full diagnosis. Each of these is the person's call; a price change for existing customers is hard to undo (the decision-making and pricing skills help).
- **Very few customers** (about ten or fewer). Rates and percentages mean little at this size. Talk to every customer this week, and to everyone who tried and stopped; the pattern is in their stories.

## The money map, by kind of business

| Kind of business | Money in | Places it can leak | The quickest number for each |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subscription** (software, memberships, retainers) | paying customers × what each pays per month × months they stay | customers stop paying; customers pay but barely use it; too few new customers; price below the value | paying customers by month from billing; who stopped and when; last use per customer; new customers per month and where from |
| **One-off sales** (a shop, an app with purchases) | buyers × order size × how often they come back | buyers never return; few visitors become buyers; small orders; margin eaten by costs | share of last quarter's buyers who bought again; visitors and orders per week; average order |
| **Services** (studio, agency, consultancy) | clients × project size × repeat work | clients don't come back; few new leads; rates below the value; time spent that isn't billed | share of last year's clients who came back; leads per month and from where; rate per day against hours actually worked |
| **Two-sided** (a marketplace, a platform with payers and users) | the paying side × what each pays, held up by the using side | one side is missing, so the other leaves; the paying side doesn't see enough use to keep paying | active users per paying customer; which side is scarce today |

The two-sided row matters more than it looks. A bakery keeps paying for an ordering app only if its own customers order through it, so "we need more customers" may really mean "the bakeries' customers don't use it".

## The order, and why

Check the parts in this order. A fix later in the list is wasted while an earlier part leaks, like pouring water into a bucket with a hole.

| Order | The question, in plain words | Signs it is the leak | Hand over to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Do the customers who have it keep using it and paying?** | many stop in the first months; paying but rarely using; refunds; few would be "very disappointed" to lose it | `retention`, `customer-interviews`, `product-discovery` |
| 2 | **Do the right new customers find it and choose it?** | few new people hear of it; many hear, few try; trials don't turn into paying; deals die at "we'll think about it" | `gtm-plan` (or `gtm-review` if a plan exists), `positioning`, `seo`, `ai-visibility`; `growth` once customers stay |
| 3 | **Do they pay the right amount for what they get?** | customers stay and use it a lot, yet pay little; nobody ever pushes back on price; heavy users pay the same as light ones; a discount on every deal | `pricing` |
| 4 | **Does each sale cost less than it brings in?** | winning a customer costs more than they pay before leaving; serving them takes more time than they pay for | `metrics`; the costs are the team's call |

Ask part 4 only when the person means profit or cash. For "more money coming in", parts 1 to 3 are the map.

## Getting rough numbers in an hour

When the record has nothing, these give a usable picture fast. Steps 1 to 3 take about an hour; the calls take a few days to book. Rough and recent beats precise and imagined.

1. **Billing records** (the payment provider's export, invoices, a spreadsheet): for each of the last six months, how many customers paid, how many started, how many stopped. Ten minutes in a spreadsheet.
2. **The customer list:** how each current customer first heard of the product. If nobody wrote it down, the founder often knows for most of them; mark the ones they guess.
3. **Last use:** if the product keeps a last login, last order or last booking per customer, sort by it. Paying customers who haven't used it in a month are the most likely to leave next.
4. **Six short calls:** three customers who stopped, three who stayed. Two questions each: "what were you trying to get done when you started?" and, to leavers, "what happened?", to stayers, "what would you miss most if it went away?". The customer-interviews skill has the guide.

## Say where it leaks, then lead

Keep the diagnosis to a few sentences, in this shape:

> From what you told me: [fact with its source], [fact]. So the money most likely leaks at [the part, in plain words]: [one sentence why]. I'm [fairly sure / unsure] because [reason]. This week: [one step], about [time]. Want me to start on it?

Keep skill names out of what the person reads; open the skill yourself when they say yes.

Then lead. After each step, a line on what was learned and what comes next: "The six calls say bakeries leave because their regulars never switched to ordering in the app. Next: the first week of a new bakery. I'll draft what they should see on day one." Stop when the team has a plan with a measure and a date, and offer the gtm-review skill to check it each week or month.

## Worked example 1: "How can we make more money?"

Stillwater is an invented booking app for yoga studios; studios pay a monthly fee and their clients book classes through it. The founder writes: "how can we make more money?". The project holds only a brief.

**First reply:**

> Happy to help. Before ideas, let's find where the money is being lost, because the fix depends on it. The project only has the brief so far, so three quick questions (rough answers are fine, and "don't know" is useful too):
>
> 1. What would "more money" mean for you right now? a) more coming in each month, b) more left after costs, c) cash is getting tight, d) you set a target and missed it.
> 2. How many studios pay today, and how many paid three months ago?
> 3. Do most studios' clients book through Stillwater, or do many still message the studio to book?

**The founder answers:** "a) more coming in. 46 studios pay now, 51 did in July. Honestly, lots of clients still message the studios."

**Second reply:**

> Thanks, that helps a lot. You have 5 fewer paying studios than in July, so at least 5 left and new studios aren't making up for them. Your answer to question 3 is a likely reason: a studio whose clients still message to book doesn't save much time with Stillwater, so it stops paying. That makes keeping studios the first place to look, starting with getting their clients to book in the app. I'm fairly sure, though it rests on two numbers and your sense of the clients.
>
> This week, about two hours: call three studios that left and three that stayed, and ask how their clients book and what happened. I can draft the questions now and set up a small table to track who leaves and why. Want me to?

## Worked example 2: "Satışlar kötü, ne yapmalıyız?"

Tezgah, restoranların toptancıdan malzeme sipariş ettiği hayali bir uygulama; restoranlar aylık ücret ödüyor. Kurucu yazıyor: "satışlar kötü, ne yapmalıyız?". Projede sadece brief var.

**İlk cevap:**

> Hemen bakalım. Çözüm, sorunun nerede olduğuna göre tamamen değişiyor, o yüzden önce onu bulalım. Projede şimdilik sadece brief var; üç kısa soru (kabaca cevap yeter, "bilmiyorum" da işe yarar):
>
> 1. "Satışlar kötü" derken hangisi? a) yeni restoran gelmiyor, b) gelenler bir iki ay sonra bırakıyor, c) restoranlar kalıyor ama gelir az, d) bir hedef koymuştun ve tutmadı.
> 2. Bugün kaç restoran ödüyor, üç ay önce kaç tane ödüyordu?
> 3. Son beş restoran sizi nasıl buldu?

**Kurucu cevaplıyor:** "a) yeni gelmiyor. 30 restoran var, üç ay önce 29'du, bırakan neredeyse yok. Hepsi tanıdık üzerinden geldi."

**İkinci cevap:**

> Teşekkürler, tablo netleşti. Restoranlar kalıyor, bu iyi haber: ürün işini görüyor demek. Sorun yeni restoranların sizi bulması ve seçmesi; bugüne kadar herkes tanıdık üzerinden gelmiş, yani düzenli bir müşteri bulma yolunuz henüz yok. Bundan oldukça eminim, çünkü üç ayda neredeyse kimse bırakmamış.
>
> Bu hafta iki üç saatini ayırman yeterli: en memnun üç müşterine Tezgah'ı neden seçtiklerini sor, sonra onlara benzeyen 20 restoranlık kısa bir liste çıkaralım. İlk mesajı ve basit bir takip tablosunu ben hazırlayabilirim. Başlayayım mı?

## What the two examples show

- One round of questions, each with options, then a diagnosis with its evidence. The person never had to know the words "retention" or "go-to-market".
- The order did the work: Stillwater's leak was keeping customers, and the clue was on the users' side (clients still messaging to book), so new-customer tactics would have been wasted; Tezgah kept its customers, so finding new ones was the right place.
- Each reply ends with one step, its time and an offer to start.
